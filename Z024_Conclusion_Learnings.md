# Conclusion / Learnings

### [Previous Chapter](Z022_Action_Plan.html)

Education is a combination of many different fields in a single profession.

There's also plenty of management skills involved. You can continually improve your teaching over time. The beginning may seem complicated and overwhelming, but it gets easier over time to teach subjects, create curricula, and work with people.

There's a plethora of information I may have skipped over or omitted you may experience as a teacher or instructor. The finer details vary wildly amongst schools, districts, organizations providing the continuing education, and more. There are also tips and tricks you'll discover as you get real-world, practical experience that a textual manual like this doesn't convey the information well enough until you, well, "experience" it yourself.

Outside of that, thank you for reading, and I hope you came away from this manual with a deeper understanding of the world of teaching.

### **"I Have Limited Time" Summary of Summaries**

Here's the broad summary across every chapter in this book, including some actionable items, listed in no particular order:
- **This book will NOT solve all of your problems in education.**
    - Hopefully it solves some problems though. I'll consider my goal met if that's the case.
- Your first plan will not survive initial contact. Even the advice, suggestions, and frameworks provided here may need modifications for your specific situation(s). Expect to modify plans and systems as time marches on.
- Most communications should be clear, concise, and descriptive.
- If a rule, regulation, guideline, etc. is in a system and sounds dumb, there's a good chance that rule was written in blood and has a reason "why" behind it.
- Many classroom issues are solved by three methods:
    1. Consistently (and actually) enforcing discipline and consequences for actions
    2. Allowing students to fail
    3. Limiting classroom size/number of students instructors manage
- Perfect is the enemy of good. Sometimes "good enough" is *good enough* and you need to accept that.
- If suddenly thrusted into managing a classroom with little to no preparation (and perhaps against your will, who knows), focus first on setting up classroom management and procedures, followed by a syllabus and lesson plans. A safe, secure, and orderly environment is needed *before* students may receive a proper education.
- The first year for just about every professional role people go into, especially for educators, will be hard. That is normal.
- Leadership starts from the top. A failure from above quite often leads into failures below.
- Self-regulation in practice is often difficult for students without compromising their education.
- Don't be an asshole.
- You cannot "build relationships" with people that do not want to have them, despite your best efforts. There is a certain point you'll have to accept failure here.
- Prepare for the worst, but assume the best of people and students.
- Standards are a *good* thing, but too many standards bottleneck education while too few standards compromise education.
- Apathy is the death of many things. Education included.
- Education systems require proper *management* of students. If you cannot manage students, you cannot provide them a proper education.
- Having enough resources (i.e. wealth) solves most problems inside *and outside* of education systems, even when considering diminishing returns.
- Almost all data and information you work with and see is affected by bias.
- You cannot neglect critical thinking and quantitative/qualitative reasoning. Students need to know the "why" and not just the "how" to truly learn about something.
- You can broadly categorize task execution in education through lectures, activities/projects, and tests/review.
- If a teacher/educator/etc., initially plan classrooms as if you're the only educator there and you have no backup to rely on. Don't architect a plan including aides and assistants at first unless *absolutely* necessary.
- Orientation (i.e. passporting information) may take an extremely long time, up to months and *years*, and is critical to success for onboarding professionals. Nevertheless, do not neglect it.
- Technology is dangerous and is *still* dangerous even when handled properly. The same goes for many technologies and advancements in general, such as AI (Artificial Intelligence), electricity, and a crowbar.
- Security should not be sacrificed for the sake of convenience.
- Stamina is often traded for engagement.
- You should never automate a bad process and you need backups in case of emergencies. Education in general is a *messy and highly complicated process* you should not fully automate away.
- Education can be improved through incorporating concepts from many fields, both expected and unexpected, such as engineering, architecture, design, communication, etc.
- Scaling an organization, system, business, operation, event, etc. without a proper plan, consideration for the environment, and resource/supports is the same as handling unprepared power: a poison.
- If students can get away with any form of cheating to meet your metric(s), such as earning a high grade, passing a test, etc., *they will do it.* Cheating has always happened, and will continue to happen, regardless of any technology or method used to do so.
- Every person involved in education systems (parents, administrators, educators, students, support staff, etc.) has a role to play in education's success and may contribute towards any problems it has. It doesn't matter how good classroom management is; a failure in any part is a risk towards a system failure as a result.
    - Example 1: One disruptive student can willingly, and knowingly, hinder the education of every other student.
    - Example 2: Administrators can compromise the "authority" of educators managing students and harm education.
    - Example 3: One educator can choose not to follow the same rules as other educators in a system, undermining their own position and other educator's positions, resulting in a risk to compromising education.
    - Example 4: Parents may put pressure on administrators to let students not receive consequences for their actions, increasing the risk they negatively affect that student's, and other student's, education as a result.
